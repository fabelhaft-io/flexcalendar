### Required fixes (run 2 does these and nothing else)

1. **The stack suite is broken by the test, not the code** (`test/integration/occurrences.test.mjs`). (a) `calendar()` sends `metaEnvelope: Buffer.alloc(60, 0xab)`: no header, so the live API answers `422` and both tests stop there. Use the header of `envelopeOf` in `test/integration/events.test.mjs` (first byte `1`, epoch as `uint32` at offset 1, at least 45 bytes). (b) With that fixed, the Berlin test still fails: it expects `start.local` to be `'20261024T080000Z'` and `'20261027T070000Z'`; `local` has no `Z` (`'20261024T080000'`). Everything the route answered in that test was right.
2. **`UNTIL` drops the last instance in a zone behind UTC (reachable now).** Live repro on the stack: a New York `FREQ=WEEKLY;UNTIL=20261024T140000Z` from `20261010T100000` answers 2 of its 3 instances; the one at exactly `UNTIL` is missing. Cause: the header comment says `ical.js` reads a `TZID` start as floating. It does not when the object embeds its `VTIMEZONE` (every object this API writes): `startDate.zone.tzid` is then the real zone, and `ical.js` compares the floating `UNTIL` this module writes (`localOf`) with true instants. Any instance within twice the offset before `UNTIL` is dropped for a zone behind UTC (Berlin and Kolkata hide it). The same split shows without an embedded definition: **a `Z` `RDATE`/`EXDATE` on a zoned master** is placed as if local (an `RDATE`) or never matches (an `EXDATE`). My brief asked for this: *expand a floating working copy*. Do it: on a copy of the parsed object remove every `VTIMEZONE` and every `TZID` parameter, convert a `Z` `RDATE`/`EXDATE`/`UNTIL` of a zoned master to its wall clock with `localOf`, then let `ical.js` iterate floating values; instants still come only from `src/zones.mjs`. The results must be identical with and without an embedded `VTIMEZONE`. Also fix, in the same place: an `RDATE` equal to a rule instance is one occurrence, not two; an `RDATE` after `UNTIL` stays (`UNTIL` bounds the rule only; today the `past` test drops it); a floating master's override removes its instance in a non-UTC `tz` (the key compares an instant with a wall clock).
   - Tests (mutants `UNTIL not tested against the instant` and `UNTIL not converted` both survive today): `UNTIL` equal to, one second before and one second after an instance for New York, São Paulo, Kolkata, Chatham and Berlin, each with and without the embedded `VTIMEZONE`; a `Z` `EXDATE` and a `Z` `RDATE` on a Berlin master; an `RDATE` equal to a rule instance; an `RDATE` after `UNTIL`; a floating master with an override and `tz=America/New_York`.
3. **`calendar-query` with a `time-range` makes the whole calendar a 502 (reachable now).** `sabre/vobject` stops after 3,500 instances (the service answers `REPORT 500`) and the adapter maps that to 502. Live repro: an unbounded `FREQ=DAILY` from `20161001T080000Z` plus a window in 2026-10 → 502 for every window past instance 3,500, other events included; so does an hourly series after 146 days. My padded range was the cause (brief item 2). List the whole collection instead: `listObjects(calendarId)` without a range sends the `VCALENDAR`/`VEVENT` `comp-filter` only (keep the ranged form, its tests and the adapter's boundary checks), the route stops padding, and the module's exact filter decides. Tests: route (the double records no range), and in the stack a `20161001T080000Z` daily series with a 2026-10 window answers 200 and lists its instances, and an hourly series from 2020 is `unsupported` (`too-many-instances`) while the rest of the calendar is answered.
4. **The cost of an old series blocks the process.** Measured here: an unbounded daily series from 1971 and a one-month window takes 1.1 s, an hourly Berlin series from 2022 takes 1.8 s, a series that reaches the 50,000 bound about 2 s, all synchronous on the one event loop. Most of it is ICU (`instantOf`) for instances that cannot reach the window. Skip the zone work for instances far before the window (compare the wall clock with the window's, two days of margin each side) and keep the iteration for `ical.js`. Targets I will measure: daily Berlin from 1971 ≤ 250 ms, hourly Berlin from 2022 ≤ 600 ms, and nothing may change in the answers (the oracle and the corpus decide). A test that counts zone calls beats a timing assertion; if you use time, make it generous and say so.
5. **A surviving mutant: a stored all-day time placed by UTC midnights** (an all-day override or a single all-day event with `tz` other than `UTC`) passes every test. Add the case (an all-day override of an all-day series, and a single all-day event, with `tz=Pacific/Kiritimati` and `Pacific/Pago_Pago`).
